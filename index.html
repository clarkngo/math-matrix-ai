An AI tutor that explains math confidently and wrongly does more harm than no tutor at all. These rules apply to every AI feature in the course: the tutor, the simulator coach, the decomposition narrator, and the notebook hints.

Math is verified, not trusted

Symbolic claims are checked with a computer algebra system (SymPy). Numbers are computed in your browser, and the AI only interprets them. Verified equations carry a ✓ badge.

Hints before answers

The tutor asks you to predict or explain a step before it explains it. Full solutions unlock only after real attempts.

Grounded in the page

The AI sees the module's definitions, its known misconceptions, and the current state of the visual you're using. It doesn't answer from a bare question.

One notation everywhere

Column vectors, numerator-layout Jacobians, and θ for parameters, in every lesson and every AI reply.

The AI can still be wrong

Every reply has a "report an error" button. Reported errors feed the evaluation set that every prompt change is tested against.

Your data stays yours

Personal details are removed before anything reaches the model. Research logging is opt-in and covered by IRB review.

Span, independence, basis and dimension, kernel and image, rank–nullity, and change of basis. Ends with why duplicated features make a regression design matrix singular.

Module 3Analytic Geometry
Norms, Inner Products & Orthogonality
ℓ1, ℓ2 and ℓ∞ unit balls, general inner products, Cauchy–Schwarz, angles and orthogonality. Explains from a picture why the ℓ1 ball's corners make Lasso solutions sparse.

Module 4Analytic Geometry
Orthonormal Basis & Orthogonal Projections
Gram–Schmidt, projection matrices, and QR. The key result: least-squares regression is an orthogonal projection of y onto the column space of X.
