Gradient Descent Flight Simulator

Run gradient descent on a stretched, rotated bowl and find the exact learning rate where it stops converging and starts to explode.

← Course Navigation ← Prototype notes in the research plan

Gradient descent repeats one step: $\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{x}_k - \eta\,\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_k)$. The loss here is the quadratic $f(\mathbf{x}) = \tfrac12\mathbf{x}^\top A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}^\top\mathbf{x}$, where $A$ has eigenvalues $\lambda_{\min}=1$ and $\lambda_{\max}=10$, rotated 30°. That makes the bowl ten times steeper in one direction than the other, which is what most real loss surfaces look like near a minimum.

Before you touch the controls, predict: if $\eta = 0.15$ converges, what happens at $\eta = 0.21$?

What this teaches

One gradient step multiplies the error by $(I - \eta A)$. Along each eigenvector of $A$, that is a scalar multiplier $1 - \eta\lambda_i$, so the error shrinks only if $|1 - \eta\lambda_i| < 1$ for every direction:

$$0 < \eta < \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}} = 0.2, \qquad \eta^\star = \frac{2}{\lambda_{\min} + \lambda_{\max}} \approx 0.182, \qquad \text{rate} = \frac{\kappa - 1}{\kappa + 1} \approx 0.818.$$

The steepest direction sets the ceiling, and the flattest direction sets the speed. A learning rate small enough to keep the steep direction stable makes slow progress along the flat one, and the path zig-zags. That is why feature scaling, which shrinks the condition number $\kappa$, speeds up training in practice. In the full platform, an AI coach reads the numbers in the panel above and explains which regime you're in, without computing anything itself.
